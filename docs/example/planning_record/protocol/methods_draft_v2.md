| **Type.** A scoping review maps every study that meets your question: which parts of the weight were measured, how, in whom, and where trials are missing. A systematic review then judges and sums up each part of the weight that reaches 3 or more similar randomized trials. | you (summarized) |

| **Selecting studies.** You alone screen titles and abstracts and read full texts; you decide each record, with my suggested decision beside it (my suggestion: as now). Later you screen a random tenth of all your decisions again (257 so far [19]), drawn by code, without seeing your first decision, about 2 weeks later, and report how often the two passes agree (my suggestions; the 2-week gap as one 2026 review did, #1). My reading of the 88 abstracts: none measures what such a re-check catches. | you; [16] |
| **Collecting data.** You alone fill the extraction form you approved, with my suggested values and their exact passages beside each (my suggestion). The form needs no change for these methods. | your approved form |
| **Judging each study** (systematic part only). Risk of bias for each outcome: trials with RoB 2, the revised tool for randomized trials (#5); cohort studies with ROBINS-I, its counterpart for non-randomized studies (#6) (my suggestion); cohort studies are judged and described there (you). Judging one study with ROBINS-I took from 27.8 minutes to 7 hours in published studies (#11-#13). | you; [16]; [25] |
| **Pooling.** For each part of the weight, a meta-analysis only if 3 or more similar randomized trials report it; otherwise results tabled and described part by part, reported with SWiM [20]. How "similar" is judged, and how to pool: settled if a part reaches 3 (my suggestion). Cohort studies do not count toward the 3; in the systematic part they are judged for bias and described (you). | you; [20] |
| FACT: counting randomized trials only (by title, abstract or record type), your 3 included trials name fat 1, body water 1, function 1; with your unsure ones, 12 trials: fat 4, body water 5, function 3, lean 1 [18]. | [18] |
| **Trust in each result.** Certainty for each part with GRADE, a standard way to rate how far evidence can be trusted, where trials start high and cohort studies low (#7) (my suggestion). | [16] |
| **Asian people.** Results for Asian participants described apart. | your approved question |
| **Reporting.** Scoping part with PRISMA-ScR, systematic part with PRISMA 2020: reporting checklists for each kind (#9, #10) (my suggestion). | [16] |
| **Limitations.** One person screens, reads and judges. One reviewer's full-text screening missed relevant studies in a 2026 study (#2); raters agreed only slightly on overall risk of bias in 2020 (#3). | you; [16] |
